### Contributing to the Profession
There are several ways you can contribute to your profession after graduation:
1. **Join Professional Organizations**
Groups like the IEEE, ACM or ISACA offer opportunities for networking, continuing education, and professional development.
2. **Publish Research.**
If you have done any research during your studies, consider publishing it in academic journals or presenting it at conferences.
3. **Mentor Others.** Share your knowledge by mentoring current students or new graduates.
4. **Volunteer.** Offer your skills to non-profit organizations or open-source projects that align with your interests.
*Research -> Facebook? / Local organizations?*

### Building a Portfolio
1. **Security Audits.** Document your experience with security audits or vulnerability assessments.
2. **Penetration Testing Reports.** Include (anonymized) reports from penetration testing exercises.
3. **Secure Coding Practices.** Examples of code you've written that demonstrates best practices in security.
4. **Security Tools.** Any tools of scripts you've developed to automate security tasks.
5. **Case Studies.** Detailed descriptions of security projects you've worked on, including the problem, your approach, and the outcome.
---
### Where and How to Build Portfolio
* **Personal Website.** Use platforms like Github Pages, Wix or Squarespace to create a professional-looking website.
*Build or pay to have it built, might learn more from building it yourself.*
* **Github.** Host your code and projects on Github to showcase your technical skills.
* **LinkedIn.** Share your portfolio link on your LinkedIn profile to make it easily accessible to potential employers.

### Programming
* **Languages.** Python, Javascript, C++, Java and Go are popular for security.
* **Resources.** CodeAcademy, Coursera, edX, and FreeCodeCamp offer courses in various programming languages.,
### Electronic Engineering
* **Topics.** Digital systems, circuit design, embedded systems.
* **Resources.** MIT OpenCourseware, Khan Academy, and online courses from platforms like Coursera and Udacity.
### Bug Bounties
* **Platforms.** HackerOne, Bugcrowd, and Intigriti offer bug bounty programs.
* **Resources.** HackerOne's Hacker101, "The Web Application Hacker's Handbook" by Dafydd Stuttard and Marcus Pinto.
### Law and Policy
* **Topics.** Cybersecurity law, data protection regulations (like GDPR), and ethical hacking policies.
* **Resources.** Coursera, edX, and books like "Computer Crime Law" by Orin S. Kerr.
### Finding Resources
* **Online Courses.** Platforms like Coursera, edX, Udacity, and Pluralsight offer a wide range of courses.
* **Books.** Amazon, local libraries, and online retailers for specialized books.
* **Community Forums.** Reddit (e.g. r/AskNetsec, r/learnprogramming), Stack Overflow, and specialized forums like the OWASP community.
